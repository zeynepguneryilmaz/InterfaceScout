# Methods | software verification for InterfaceScout v1.1.0-rc1

## Software and declared model

InterfaceScout implements 11 residue-chemistry compatibility maps and descriptive coarse surface-region ranking in a local FastAPI/browser application. Atom-level solvent-accessible surface area is computed with Shrake–Rupley sampling using a 1.40 Å probe and 200 surface points per atom. Side-chain solvent exposure is normalized using fixed amino-acid reference areas, with exposed sites defined as scRSA ≥ 0.05. Local chemistry-compatible contributions are aggregated at 6 Å and 9 Å; coarse candidate patches use an 8 Å Cα neighborhood. Patch ranking applies Pareto dominance to four explicitly defined descriptors (chemistry support, accessibility, multiscale patch coherence and coarse orientation coherence). The compatibility rules and scientific model version (`2.4.0-selected-parameters`) are unchanged from the frozen publication model. The version bump identifies reader/export/precision/correspondence updates.

## Structural preprocessing and correspondence

Input is PDB format. The first model is analyzed, either for all protein chains or a user-selected subset. Standard protein heavy atoms are retained; hydrogens and hetero/noncanonical residues are omitted under an explicit, documented simplification. Alternative atom locations are resolved consistently at residue level by heavy-atom occupancy, with ambiguity recorded. Missing Cα and duplicated first-model atom identities cause explicit errors; incomplete side chains/backbones, omitted noncanonical components and unused additional models generate warnings. Different author residue numbers and chain letters are treated as *labels*, not physicochemical features. For structure-to-structure comparison, chain-aware global sequence alignment identifies one-to-one chemically identical positions. Insufficient coverage or ambiguous repeated-chain assignments are rejected or flagged. Geometry-dependent scores are independently computed for each provided structure; correspondence is used only for descriptions of differences and frequency, not to fit the maps.

## Numerical verification

The acceptance suite specifies **21 input conditions × 11 chemistry channels = 231 maps**, including versioned structure snapshots, pH perturbations and raw RCSB PDB examples. Each input and the declarative model specification are protected by SHA-256. The program recomputes all SASA/chemistry maps and writes machine-readable per-condition results. A separately coded scalar oracle checks per-residue chemistry, spatial density, patch descriptors, membership, Pareto ordering and score exports against an explicitly declared tolerance contract. Two raw small-protein fixtures additionally use an independently implemented point-occlusion SASA calculation. Isolated-atom, radius-boundary, exposure-threshold and mutation tests detect analytical or implementation mistakes; deliberate negative tests demonstrate that the validator can fail. Full floating-point quantities are retained until presentation or fixed-width B-factor serialization. Near ties are diagnosed; tiny numerical differences are not assigned biological significance.

## Reproducibility, platforms and end-to-end operation

GitHub Actions installs pinned Python dependencies under Python 3.10, 3.11 and 3.12 on Ubuntu, Windows and macOS. Dedicated jobs test actual first-run scripts, REST endpoints, a real Chromium/3Dmol interface, single and paired PDB analyses, independent map screenshots and complete ZIP exports. Large-input stress includes a bundled protein structure of 2,626 modeled residues, and a separately deposited GAPDH tetramer (PDB 1U8F) with approximately 1,332 protein residues, checking expected assembly-dependent burial and complete exports. Browser-export validation checks 11 PDF documents with real viewer imagery, separate screenshots, map-labeled B-factor PDBs, all-residue tables and consensus/comparison reports. The generated artifact ZIPs and source/checksum manifests are preserved as CI evidence. Some large-structure/stress PDF screenshots are synthetic *test input images* to validate PDF serialization, not evidence of rendered molecular views; browser-smoke exports contain actual 3Dmol renderings.

## Historical scientific comparison (separate evidence)

The frozen seven primary and two challenge literature benchmark cases, together with the eight-protein label-free parameter-selection set, are retained separately. Running `validation/verify_publication.py` verifies the historical recorded aggregate metrics; it does **not** independently recreate the experimental literature labels or establish new adsorption predictive performance. Because the numerical implementation now retains more precision, the publication candidate may have a small number of changed low-rank patches relative to old frozen outputs. All numerical claims should be based on the recomputed, version-specific 231-map record and explicit before/after audit.

## Statistical interpretation

The verification tests software determinism and the numerical implementation of declared geometry/chemistry operators; they do not estimate affinity, adsorption probability, preferred atomistic pose, adsorption kinetics or dynamics. Descriptive consensus percentages across user-selected structures are frequencies in those supplied files, not thermodynamic populations. A change of protein assembly or conformer may physically change SASA, and is not a nuisance label transformation. Computational correctness is necessary but not sufficient for prospective experimental utility.
